title: 'Nicola Messina: A journey through fine-grained vision-language understanding
  under limited supervision'
description: 'Recent vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities
  in capturing global visual semantics, yet they often struggle to understand fine-grained
  details. This is especially true in scenarios where we lack fine-grained training
  data, and we have to rely on weakly-supervised methodologies. This talk will be
  devoted to the exploration of the different dimensions of &quot;fine-grained&quot;
  perception: from the semantic nuances (i.e., distinguishing a wooden chair from
  a plastic one) up to spatial awareness, where the model should be able to effectively
  embed meaningful information from specific spatial locations in the image. I will
  address these topics by traversing different downstream tasks which require fine-grained
  spatial and semantic abilities, from fine-grained open-vocabulary object detection
  to zero-shot segmentation and zero-shot image captioning. Within this talk, I will
  examine the current limitations of large-scale pretrained models like CLIP in solving
  such problems, and I will propose possible solutions ranging from specialized benchmarks
  and datasets to novel architectures. The proposed approaches aim to address the
  &quot;blind spots&quot; of current models through targeted fine-tuning of existing
  multimodal backbones or by leveraging recent promising self-supervised vision models,
  paving the way towards more generalizable and data-efficient methods.'
