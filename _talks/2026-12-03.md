title: 'Miguel Costa: Learning when and where to adapt: Reinforcement learning for
  climate-resilient urban transport'
description: 'Cities need to decide now which climate adaptation measures to build,
  where to put them, and when, while facing deep uncertainty about how the climate
  will change over the coming decades. Each investment will last for a few decades
  and will be costly. Their impacts and benefits can also play out through coupled
  systems, such as, rainfall drives flooding, flooding disrupts how people move around
  the city, creating economic and wellbeing impacts. Classical optimisation methods
  struggle at the city-scale over a planning horizon of 75 years or more. In this
  talk, I will present a decision-support framework which can address this problem
  by embedding an integrated assessment model inside a reinforcement learning (RL)
  loop. The model chains climate projections, a flood model, a transport simulation
  and an assessment of direct and indirect impacts. A graph neural network policy
  learns adaptation pathways from this model: it decides per zone and per year which
  nature-based and grey measures to deploy, balancing investment and maintenance costs
  against avoided damage, travel delays and cancelled trips. On a full-city case study
  of pluvial flooding in Copenhagen (278 zones, 2025–2100, 42 scenarios from EURO-CORDEX),
  RL cuts total costs by about 25% compared with doing nothing and outperforms competing
  alternatives across two case studies (Bayesian optimisation, CMA-ES, trigger rules,
  Copenhagen’s own Cloudburst Plan design standard, and Direct Policy Search). Results
  hold across scenarios, including on held-out climate trajectories. I will then discuss
  how such a framework can support policymakers in making informed decisions and ongoing
  extensions of this work and opportunities for broader application, including optimization
  for non-economic impacts (e.g., quality of life, wellbeing), other case studies,
  and climate impacts.'
